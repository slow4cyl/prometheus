Method notes

  1. Communities are detected by greedy modularity optimisation (Clauset–Newman–Moore style) on the routing graph itself. They group domains that exchange inquiry, not domains that share a textbook shelf — a domain can sit in a “medical” community purely because it trades follow-up questions with medical domains.
  2. An edge s → t aggregates routing events in which work in s queued follow-up inquiry in t (queue additions, transfer suggestions); its weight is the event count. Sources: prometheus.db and the classifier routing logs.
  3. Verdicts come from worker hypothesis_supported flags: confirmed / refuted / unknown. Confirm rates exclude unknowns.
  4. Transfer outcomes are counted per edge where transfer evidence was recorded; confirm rate = confirmed ÷ (confirmed + refuted). Centroid-fallback edges are attribution guesses and carry no scored outcomes.
  5. Raw confidence is what workers self-report (hard-capped at 0.85 by the write path); calibrated confidence re-prices results against historical outcome frequencies. Averages are weighted by per-domain result volume.
  6. Claim tiers are the promotion ladder: CANDIDATE → REPLICATED (≥1 independent retest) → ESTABLISHED (survives an adversarial replication attack). DISPUTED claims carry live contradictions and an active arbitration path. Attack outcomes are a trichotomy — survived / narrowed / broken; a NARROWED result is a partial break that spawns a boundary-mapping question and deliberately counts toward neither pole, so survival rate is survived ÷ (survived + broken). Meta-claims (self-measurement) are excluded.
  7. Gini coefficient: 0 = perfectly even attention, 1 = a single domain holds everything. Computed exactly over per-domain values.
  8. Fruchterman–Reingold force embedding with community-ring seeding and overlap relaxation, computed in Python at report-build time (seed 42). No JavaScript layout engine, no CDN — the file is fully self-contained and works offline.
  9. A hub is any domain holding >5% of total experiments.
  10. Momentum counts experiments whose start time falls in the trailing 7 / 30 days at export-build time; heat is the 7-day count as a share of the domain's lifetime experiments — high heat marks a young or resurgent domain, low heat a legacy stock.
